# 7. Limitations and Future Work

## 7.1 Limitations

**Dataset size.** The evaluation uses 26 receipts from a single R&D project. While this size is sufficient for qualitative findings, directional metrics, and hypothesis-driven analysis, it limits the statistical power of Cohen's κ estimates and field-level precision scores. Confidence intervals on κ values computed from 26 samples are wide; a small number of disagreements can produce large swings in the reported metric. Results should therefore be interpreted as directional evidence rather than definitive performance benchmarks.

**Single domain and single project.** All 26 receipts originate from one R&D project in the technology sector. The policy, the receipt categories, and the vendor landscape are all consistent with a software/AI research context. Generalization to other domains—medical research expenses, construction project receipts, retail procurement, academic grant reporting—is not demonstrated. In particular, the self-referential design of this study (receipts from building an AI auditing system evaluated against the policy for that same project) means that the LLM may have prior knowledge of what constitutes an appropriate R&D expense in this context, potentially inflating accuracy relative to less familiar domains.

**Synthetic policy.** The project policy was designed specifically for this study. While the three variants represent a meaningful spectrum of policy quality, real-world expense policies exhibit additional dimensions of variation: they may reference external regulatory frameworks (e.g., EU grant regulations, tax law), use highly domain-specific terminology, contain implicit exceptions based on organizational context, or exist across multiple documents rather than a single JSON file. The contradictory policy variant in this study embeds obvious contradictions within a single document; real-world policy contradictions are often subtler, arising from cross-document inconsistencies or from policy changes that were not fully propagated.

**Single annotator.** Human verdicts were assigned by one annotator. Inter-annotator agreement was not measured, which limits the reliability of Cohen's κ as a gold standard. Expense justification is inherently subjective; different reviewers with different organizational experience may reach different verdicts on the same receipts. Without an inter-annotator agreement study, it is not possible to separate model disagreement with the human annotator from genuine annotation noise. This is the most significant methodological limitation of the study.

**No fine-tuning baseline.** We do not compare zero-shot CoT performance against a fine-tuned classifier. For a fair comparison, a fine-tuned model would require a substantially larger labeled dataset than 26 receipts. However, the absence of a fine-tuning baseline means that we cannot quantify the performance gap between zero-shot prompting and a supervised approach, nor demonstrate whether the pipeline offers advantages in low-data regimes.

**Temperature and reproducibility.** API-accessed models are not fully deterministic even at temperature=0. Minor differences in infrastructure, batch processing, and model version updates can produce different outputs on the same input across time. The reported results reflect a single run of each experiment; repeated runs may produce slightly different κ values.

**PDF handling limitation.** The current implementation processes only the first page of multi-page PDFs. Several receipts in the dataset are multi-page invoices where the total amount appears on a page other than the first. These receipts are either excluded from quantitative analysis or manually corrected, which limits the generalizability of the E1 OCR precision results.

**Prompt sensitivity.** LLM performance is sensitive to prompt phrasing, and the prompts used in this study were not extensively ablated. Alternative formulations of the four-step CoT instruction, different output schemas, or different system role descriptions may produce different results. The study should be understood as evaluating one specific prompt design rather than the general capability of LLMs on this task.

## 7.2 Future Work

**Larger and more diverse dataset.** A rigorous evaluation of the pipeline requires a dataset with at minimum several hundred annotated receipts spanning multiple domains, organizations, and policy types. Multi-annotator annotation with inter-annotator agreement computation (using the Krippendorff's alpha or Fleiss's κ) would provide a reliable gold standard. An expanded dataset would also enable stratified analysis: performance broken down by expense category, receipt quality tier, language, and vendor type.

**RAG-based policy retrieval.** Real-world expense policies are often long, multi-section documents stored in policy management systems, contract repositories, or HR knowledge bases. Injecting the full policy text into every prompt is not scalable for long documents and may cause context window management problems. A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approach—retrieving the most relevant policy clauses based on the expense category before constructing the verdict prompt—would be more scalable and would also test whether clause-level retrieval improves or degrades verdict accuracy compared to full-policy injection.

**Fine-tuning on labeled data.** With a sufficiently large annotated dataset, fine-tuning a smaller open-weight model (such as a 7B or 13B parameter model) for the verdict classification task would enable deployment on local infrastructure, reducing per-query cost and eliminating API dependency. The comparison between fine-tuned small models and zero-shot large models is a natural follow-on study.

**Multi-language systematic evaluation.** The current pipeline handles Russian and German receipts through the models' multilingual capabilities, but no systematic analysis of language-specific accuracy is performed. A dedicated evaluation on receipts in each target language, with language-specific ground truth annotation, would establish whether the pipeline is deployable in a multilingual accounting context—an important practical requirement for DACH-region organizations.

**Real-world policy integration.** Rather than using a synthetic policy document, connecting the pipeline to live policy sources—project management tools (e.g., JIRA project settings), contract management systems, or HR policy repositories—would test whether the pipeline can handle the full complexity of organizational policy documents. This would also require handling policy versioning: expenses incurred under a previous policy version must be evaluated against the policy in force at the time of the expense.

**Inter-annotator agreement study.** Before deploying this pipeline in a production audit context, an inter-annotator agreement study on a representative receipt sample would establish the human performance ceiling—the level of agreement that can realistically be expected between two qualified human reviewers. This baseline would allow the Cohen's κ results from the LLM pipeline to be contextualized: a κ close to the human ceiling would indicate that the LLM is performing as well as a second human reviewer.

**Adversarial receipt inputs.** This study uses genuine receipts from real transactions. A complementary study should test the pipeline on adversarially constructed receipts: forged documents, receipts with manipulated amounts, duplicate receipts, and receipts from non-existent vendors. Document forgery detection is a distinct capability from semantic policy compliance, but understanding the pipeline's behavior on manipulated inputs is important for any deployment in a financial control context.

**Cost-accuracy trade-off analysis.** A formal analysis of the cost per verdict at each model tier—combined with the accuracy results from E2 and E3—would provide a decision framework for model selection in production deployments. The optimal model may not be the highest-accuracy one if a lower-cost model achieves acceptable accuracy on the majority of receipts and routes only edge cases to the more expensive model.
